Open firmware · EleksTube IPS Gen2

Six little screens,
your own clock.

Dekatron turns the EleksTube IPS clock into something you can shape: a web page in the clock, themes that cross the six tubes, faces from any font, and updates over the air. You install it by pasting a prompt into an AI assistant.

Work in progress. The first public release is not out yet. The code, the formats and the creator are here and work; the installer and the downloads arrive with v0.1.

Does it work on my clock?

Yes

EleksTube IPS Gen2 (also sold as G2.1): six 135 × 240 IPS screens, an ESP32 PICO-D4 with 4 MB of flash, six RGB lights, a DS3231 clock and four buttons.

Not tried

The first EleksTube (glowing tubes or OLED), the Gen1 IPS without Wi-Fi, and other clones. If yours has the same parts on other pins, copy firmware/boards/elekstube-ips-gen2.yaml and send it as a pull request.

Dekatron is compatible with the EleksTube IPS clocks and has no affiliation with EleksTube.

01 Install

1 · On the clock

Plug it into your computer with a USB cable and keep its original power supply at hand. Nothing in the clock needs to be opened.

2 · On your computer

Open an AI assistant that can use the terminal, such as Claude Code, and paste the prompt. It checks the computer, finds the clock, copies its flash first, and asks you what it needs.

3 · Follow along

Choose a theme, give it your Wi-Fi, and open the page of the clock. To go back to the original firmware, ask the assistant to restore the copy.

The installer comes with the first release. The prompt, tools/install.py and the downloads of the firmware are being written, so there is nothing to paste yet. Everything else on this page already works. Watch the repository for v0.1.

02 Use

The page of the clock

Open the address of the clock in a browser, on a phone or a computer on the same network. It has three tabs: Clock (brightness, lights, night mode, faces, countdown), Files (upload a theme, a face, a backup or a firmware, and see what is on the clock) and Settings (timezone, password, firmware).

Wi-Fi

A clock without Wi-Fi shows a code on its screens. Scan it with your phone, choose your network, and the clock joins it.

Buttons

Left and right change the face, Mode starts a countdown, Power turns the screens off, and holding Mode for ten seconds forgets the Wi-Fi.

03 Update

The clock never goes to the internet. Its page does: when you open it, at most every two hours, it asks this site whether there is a newer firmware, and shows a dot on Settings if so. Update downloads the firmware here, checks its SHA-256 and sends it to the clock, which restarts and goes back to the old firmware if the new one does not start. You can also upload a .ota.bin yourself in Files.

04 Themes

A theme is four animations that cross the six tubes, with lights that follow the picture: at startup, while connecting, when connected and at the end of a countdown. Download the file, then upload it in Files on the page of your clock. These pages are served over HTTPS and cannot reach the clock themselves, which speaks plain HTTP.

05 Create

The creator runs in your browser and sends nothing anywhere. Give it an animated GIF, WebP or PNG, a video or a series of pictures for each of the four animations and it makes the theme file, with a preview of the six tubes. For clock faces, pick a font or ten pictures.

06 Create with an AI

The tools of the repository make the same files from the command line, and check them against the rules of the clock, so an assistant can do it for you and fix its own mistakes. The details are in docs/making-bins.md.

A theme, with an assistant that has a terminal

Read docs/making-bins.md in this repository. Make me a theme called "Surf" for the Dekatron clock: waves and a surfboard, in warm colors, four short animations (boot, connecting, online, countdown_end) of 1370 x 240 pixels at 15 fps, drawn by you with Python and Pillow. Build it with tools/make_theme.py, license CC-BY-SA-4.0, author "Me". Run tools/check_bin.py --gallery and fix every problem until there is none. Give me the path of the .bin.

A face from a font

Read docs/making-bins.md in this repository. Make me a clock face called "Neon" with the font at ./MyFont.ttf in blue, with tools/make_face.py, license CC-BY-SA-4.0, author "Me". Check it with tools/check_bin.py --gallery and give me the path of the .bin.

With a chat that has no terminal

I am making an animation for a clock with six small screens side by side. Make four short animated GIFs, each exactly 1370 x 240 pixels at 15 frames per second, black background, a style of waves and surfboards in warm colors: 1) the clock starting (8 s), 2) looking for Wi-Fi (6 s, can loop), 3) connected (4 s), 4) the end of a countdown (6 s). The picture crosses the six screens, which are 135 pixels wide and 112 pixels apart, so keep the action inside them. Few colors and flat shapes.

Then open the creator, give it the four GIFs and download the theme.

07 Is it safe?

Your copy comes first

The original firmware is tied to the clock's address and cannot be downloaded again. The installer reads the whole flash twice, compares the two, and does not go on without a copy you can restore.

One command back

The copy is written back with one command and the clock is as it was. This has been tried on a real clock.

If an update fails

A firmware that does not start is dropped, and the clock goes back to the previous one by itself. Updates need a password, and every file is checked before it is used.
